The rules

How exposure is set

Full methodology →

US stocks

  • Bullish · 100%. The composite score is above −0.10. It also goes back to 100% early after a sell-off: when SPY reclaims a rising 50-day average after a 20% decline, or rallies 10% off its 20-session low while inflation is under 4%.
  • Neutral · 50%. The score is below −0.10, but the macro signals have not confirmed it.
  • Bearish · 0%. The score is below −0.10 and macro confirms it. Cash earns T-bills.

Bitcoin

  • Bullish · 100%. The composite score is above +0.20, or, once Bitcoin is 50% or more below its high, it closes back above its 20-week average.
  • Neutral · 50%. The score is between −0.15 and +0.20, or it is below −0.15 deep in a bear market without macro confirmation.
  • Bearish · 0%. The score is below −0.15. Cash earns T-bills.

High beta

  • Starts from the stock desk. High beta takes the stock desk’s state, then its own rules can cut it further; the lowest exposure wins.
  • Neutral · 50%. High beta is clearly lagging the S&P 500, or the basket falls 12% or more in 10 sessions.
  • Bearish · 0%. The stock desk is Bearish, or the basket closes below its 200-day average while the stock model is not firmly positive.
  • The basket. The 25 Nasdaq-100 stocks with the highest beta to QQQ over two years, equal weight, re-ranked each quarter. A separate, higher-risk desk, not part of the flagship.
What the numbers on these cards mean
Bullish, Neutral, Bearish
Every desk is in one of three states: Bullish (100% invested), Neutral (50%) or Bearish (0%, in T-bills). Each desk’s own rules decide which.
Composite score
A weighted average of trend, momentum, market internals, macro, volatility and calendar signals, each scaled from −1 to +1. The stock desk cuts exposure when it falls below −0.10.
Chance of a 10% drop in 6 months
How often SPY fell 10% or more within six months on past days with a similar score, since 2003.
Macro group vs the cash gate
When the stock score is below −0.10, the desk goes Bearish (cash) only if the macro signals are at or below −0.25; otherwise it goes Neutral (half invested).
Strength vs S&P 500
Whether the high-beta basket is keeping up with the S&P. Clearly lagging cuts that desk to half.
Put wall, call wall, dealer gamma
Where the most put and call open interest sits below and above the price, and an estimate of whether options dealers’ hedging tends to calm moves (positive) or amplify them (negative). Context only.
