Methodology

How the models make a call.

Every Kerdos desk follows the same discipline: read published market and economic data after the close, turn it into a score, map the score to one of three states, and size exposure from the state. There are no discretionary overrides. This page explains each step, what each desk reads, how risk is managed, when the models update, and where the method falls short.

Hypothetical models · not investment advice
01 · The process

From data to a position

The stocks and Bitcoin desks run these five steps every day. The high-beta desk starts from the stocks desk’s state and applies its own risk rules on top.

01Data

Closing prices, index levels and published economic series: Robinhood, FRED, Robert Shiller’s long-run S&P data, Coin Metrics.

02Factors

Each input becomes a signal between −1 and +1: trend, momentum, internals, macro, volatility, valuation, calendar and cycle.

03Score

Signals are averaged within groups, the groups are weighted into one composite score, and the score is smoothed over 5 days.

04State

Thresholds map the score to Risk On, Balanced or Risk Off. A new signal needs 3 straight days, to cut whipsaw.

05Exposure

Risk On holds 100%, Balanced 50%, Risk Off 0%. Money not invested earns the 3-month T-bill rate.

03 · AllocationHow the flagship is sized

The flagship portfolio adds no forecasting signals of its own. It holds fixed target sleeves and lets the desks scale them: the stocks desk sizes the stock sleeve, the Bitcoin desk sizes the Bitcoin sleeve, gold is always held, and whatever the sleeves do not hold waits in T-bills.

Loading the flagship rules…

A desk’s new call is acted on at the next close, and the portfolio is also rebalanced to its targets at the start of each month. Before 2014, when the Bitcoin desk has no history, the Bitcoin sleeve is added to the stock sleeve. Followers can hold their own stock picks in the stock sleeve, sized up and down with the stocks desk’s calls; their results will differ from the model’s.

04 · RiskHow risk is managed

Drawdowns first
The models are built to avoid large declines, then to get back in quickly after a bottom. Their scores separate the odds of a 10% decline far better than they separate returns.
Half before none
On the stocks desk, a cut signal halves exposure; it goes to cash only when the macro signals confirm it, because unconfirmed cut signals were usually dips inside bull markets.
Confirmation
Scores are smoothed over 5 days and a new signal must hold for 3 straight days, so one noisy session cannot flip a desk.
Early re-entry
Stocks: after a 20% fall, a close above a rising 50-day average, or a 10% rally off the 20-session low while CPI inflation is under 4%. Bitcoin: once 50% or more below its high, a close above the 20-week average.
Top signal
On Bitcoin, the Pi-cycle top signal caps exposure at half for 120 days after it fires.
No leverage
Published states run from 0% to 100%. The stocks desk also shows a 1.25x research variant; the flagship does not use it.
Separate satellite
The higher-risk high-beta desk is kept outside the flagship portfolio.

05 · TimingUpdates and assumptions

When the models run
Once a day, before the US open, on the latest completed session’s closing data. The Bitcoin desk uses the latest completed day and also runs on weekends.
When this site updates
Once a day, after the 4:00 p.m. ET close, by about 4:30 p.m. ET. Each update is saved as a timestamped version.
When trades are assumed
A call made at one close is traded at the next close, in the backtests and in the flagship’s live record.
Costs and income
Trading costs are included (0.05% per unit traded in the flagship). Cash earns the 3-month T-bill rate. Dividends on SPY and QQQ are approximated. No taxes.
No look-ahead
Monthly macro data enter only after their publication dates, seasonal tables use only earlier years, and cycle measures use the running high rather than hindsight peaks.
Backtest windows
Stocks from 2003, Bitcoin from 2015 (desk history from 2014), the flagship from 2005, the high-beta basket from 2018.
06 · Research integrity

Built to be checked

A public, timestamped record

Every daily update is saved as a dated version of the site, with each report stamped with the close it used and the time it was generated. What a model said, and when, cannot be revised after the fact.

Backtests labeled as backtests

Backtested results are shown as hypothetical and kept apart from the flagship’s live record. The live record never inherits the backtest’s numbers.

Rejected ideas published

Each desk page lists the rules that were tested and left out, and why, alongside the ones that were kept, so the method can be judged by its misses as well as its hits.

Cutting from 100% to 50% before a 10% fall scores +5%; doing it before a 10% rise scores −5%. Moves under 0.05% count as flat. Trend models are usually wrong more often than right and earn their keep on a few large moves, so compare the typical (median) size of each, not only the counts. T-bill interest is left out. Rows marked backtest are the current rules run over past data, not calls published at the time.

08 · LimitsWhat the models cannot do

In-sample rules
Factor directions, thresholds, the macro gate and the re-entry rules were chosen with the 2003–2026 stock history and the 2014–2026 Bitcoin history in view, so the backtests are optimistic.
Few examples
Daily stock data covers three recessions and five bear markets; Bitcoin has had only four full cycles. That is enough to see patterns, not to prove them.
Bitcoin dominates
Bitcoin accounts for most of the flagship backtest’s lead over the S&P 500 since 2015 and drives about 70% of the portfolio’s swings. Its early gains are unlikely to repeat.
Slow after V-shaped lows
On its own the stocks score is slow after sharp rebounds; the rebound rule exists for those, and it added little on older data.
Survivorship bias
The high-beta basket’s history is rebuilt from today’s Nasdaq-100 list, so it overstates what a real-time list would have earned.
News is not scored
Headlines and catalysts are shown for context only and never change a call.
Approximations
Dividends are approximated, some macro series are proxies, and prices may be delayed or contain errors.
Not advice
These are hypothetical research models. They manage no money, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.
